# 180-sites: page-by-page review

> Reference evidence (2026-10-23). For current product decisions and instructions, start with [the workspace documentation](../../../README.md). Observations, recommendations and vendor claims retain the qualifications below.

Reviewed October 23, 2026. 19 selected public pages. Each entry records retrieved content, a design judgment and a proposed Biz Da Vinci response. These are not measured conversion findings. Long testimonial collections, indexes and legal/FAQ pages were reviewed selectively as specified below. See [method](../METHOD.md), [site synthesis](README.md), [coverage](coverage.json) and [retrieval metadata](retrieval-metadata.json). Browser testing is limited to the states documented in the site synthesis.

## 1. Homepage

[Source page](https://180sites.com/)

**Observed:** A trades-oriented website subscription leads with a low monthly starting price, portfolio, process and managed service.

**What works:** A clear audience and packaged delivery reduce the buyer's planning burden.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Monthly affordability is easier to notice than the full commitment. Broad claims against freelancers are positioning, not comparative evidence.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Explain business outcomes and term total together. Use specific evidence instead of disparaging alternative providers.

## 2. Pricing

[Source page](https://180sites.com/pricing)

**Observed:** Three plans show $195/$250/$300 per month with 5/10/20 pages and a 24-month term; additional copy, pages and custom design carry charges.

**What works:** Concrete allowances and add-ons expose important scope decisions.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Base commitments are $4,680/$6,000/$7,200 before extras, a different impression from monthly price alone.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Display setup, monthly amount, minimum term, total commitment and exclusions together; establish our own delivery economics.

## 3. Audit

[Source page](https://180sites.com/audit)

**Observed:** The offer covers user experience, lead generation, SEO and performance. The $0 route is a strategy call; a $200 premium route offers video delivery in 1–4 days.

**What works:** A paid deeper review has a tangible deliverable and timeframe.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The free route is not an immediate on-screen diagnosis. References to a PDF elsewhere need reconciliation with the video package.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Give useful findings immediately; distinguish optional consultation from a report and from paid expert analysis.

## 4. Get started

[Source page](https://180sites.com/get-started)

**Observed:** The intake asks for contact, business, website and address details and promises a prompt email response.

**What works:** The next step is operationally explicit.

**Friction or uncertainty:** This is a high-information request before personalized value; it is more suitable after a decision than at first arrival.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use a minimal optional implementation request after the free results and preserve the existing result context.

## 5. How it works

[Source page](https://180sites.com/how-it-works)

**Observed:** A seven-step sequence covers agreement/payment, information, development, review, launch and care.

**What works:** Review and approval checkpoints help owners understand their responsibilities.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A 30-day message and an average 4–6-week expectation need clearer prerequisites; payment comes before the build.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Show a realistic timeline, owner inputs, review stages and what pauses the schedule.

## 6. Website management

[Source page](https://180sites.com/website-management)

**Observed:** Describes included initial management, backups, software updates, reports and monitoring.

**What works:** Makes the ongoing work behind a subscription visible.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Monitoring intervals differ between sections; 'included' depends on the paid plan and term.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Keep maintenance frequency and support promises in a single maintained service definition.

## 7. FAQs

[Source page](https://180sites.com/faqs)

**Observed:** Answers ownership after payoff, hosting during the term, cancellation options and post-term care.

**What works:** Addresses the questions owners often discover too late.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Early exit can mean paying half the remainder and losing the site or paying the balance for files. These are consequential conditions.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Expose ownership and exit terms next to prices, not only in FAQs.

## 8. Terms and conditions

[Source page](https://180sites.com/terms-and-conditions)

**Observed:** Sets the initial refund period and the two-year commercial commitment.

**What works:** A formal scope document makes the commitment discoverable.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The relationship between the contract wording and FAQ cancellation options is not immediately obvious.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Make plan, FAQ and contract language agree; do not characterize a financed build as cancel-anytime care.

## 9. Upgrades

[Source page](https://180sites.com/upgrades)

**Observed:** Separates enhancements such as content, pages, reviews and business tools from the core service.

**What works:** Optional capabilities can be introduced when relevant.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Free-with-asterisk messaging and outside costs require careful reading.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Show included features, paid add-ons and third-party charges as distinct items.

## 10. Portfolio index

[Source page](https://180sites.com/portfolio)

**Observed:** Groups website examples including custom and template-oriented work.

**What works:** A buyer can inspect actual design variety instead of relying solely on claims.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Screenshots and project count do not establish lead or revenue improvements.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use a small relevant selection, label project scope and link to the specific case.

## 11. Reviews

[Source page](https://180sites.com/reviews)

**Observed:** Presents numerous customer comments and external review-source references.

**What works:** Named experiences can address delivery and communication concerns.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The review collection was sampled for structure; authenticity and completeness were not independently verified.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use permissioned reviews with source links and relevant context, avoiding invented or borrowed testimonials.

## 12. Dental web design

[Source page](https://180sites.com/dental-web-design)

**Observed:** Uses a profession-specific pitch but retrieved text contains a $195 headline and a $150 FAQ price; portfolio links include landscaping businesses.

**What works:** Industry-specific language can help practices recognize their needs.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Price inconsistency and mismatched example links weaken relevance. The link issue was found in retrieved content, not a rendered click test.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Make medical examples genuinely relevant and feed prices from shared data; audit industry pages for template leftovers.

## 13. Car wraps web design

[Source page](https://180sites.com/car-wraps-web-design)

**Observed:** Connects visual work samples and quote requests with the buying journey for wrap businesses.

**What works:** The proposed content and conversion action fit the profession.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Portfolio quality is still not evidence that a particular design generated additional work.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Tailor proof and primary action to the industry rather than merely replacing the industry name.

## 14. House cleaning web design

[Source page](https://180sites.com/house-cleaning-web-design)

**Observed:** Combines local-service messaging, cleaning examples and lead capture.

**What works:** Relevant projects reduce the distance between the offer and the prospect's business.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Autopilot-style lead language overstates what page design alone can establish.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use service-area clarity, real cleaning examples and straightforward quote requests without guaranteed growth claims.

## 15. Crafted Stache Barbershop case

[Source page](https://180sites.com/portfolio/the-crafted-stache-barbershop)

**Observed:** Discusses visual identity, service presentation and booking-oriented design.

**What works:** Connects aesthetics to a specific customer task.

**Friction or uncertainty:** No quantified outcome is established; reusable testimonial blocks should not be mistaken for evidence about this client.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Explain what changed in the booking journey and label unmeasured outcomes honestly.

## 16. Kingdom Detailing Solutions case

[Source page](https://180sites.com/portfolio/kingdom-detailing-solutions)

**Observed:** Shows service-package presentation and a mobile-oriented inquiry journey.

**What works:** Useful reference for comparing packages and requesting detailing.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The retrieved 'Go To Website' destination points to the portfolio index rather than a specific client site; not tested as a browser click.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Keep case links accurate and pair package design with the owner's actual service workflow.

## 17. Svoma Design in Dentistry case

[Source page](https://180sites.com/portfolio/svoma-design-in-dentistry)

**Observed:** Emphasizes branding and professional presentation with a client-site link.

**What works:** A named practice and specific design rationale make the example inspectable.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A stronger appearance is not a measured increase in appointment requests.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Separate visual improvements from measured appointment outcomes and describe evidence for each.

## 18. Website audit article

[Source page](https://180sites.com/3-things-to-look-at-when-auditing-your-site)

**Observed:** An article dated February 2021 introduces design, links and SEO concerns and routes to a free call.

**What works:** Educational material can help owners understand the review vocabulary.

**Friction or uncertainty:** General advice is not a diagnosis of the visitor's website and the material is dated.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use educational explanations inside actual evidence-linked findings, with review dates.

## 19. About

[Source page](https://180sites.com/about)

**Observed:** Explains the founder's motivation, values and desire to remove website work from the owner's day.

**What works:** An identifiable team and reason for the service support trust.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Sales language should not substitute for delivery evidence.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Tell a concise authentic story and connect it to concrete service commitments.

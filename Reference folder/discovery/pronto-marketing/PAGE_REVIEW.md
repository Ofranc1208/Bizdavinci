# pronto-marketing: page-by-page review

> Reference evidence (2026-10-23). For current product decisions and instructions, start with [the workspace documentation](../../../README.md). Observations, recommendations and vendor claims retain the qualifications below.

Reviewed October 23, 2026. 19 selected public pages. Each entry records retrieved content, a design judgment and a proposed Biz Da Vinci response. These are not measured conversion findings. Long testimonial collections, indexes and legal/FAQ pages were reviewed selectively as specified below. See [method](../METHOD.md), [site synthesis](README.md), [coverage](coverage.json) and [retrieval metadata](retrieval-metadata.json). Browser testing is limited to the states documented in the site synthesis.

## 1. Homepage

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/)

**Observed:** Introduces WordPress projects, ongoing website care and marketing with multiple audience and service routes.

**What works:** The project-versus-care distinction helps explain the agency's business model.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A broad catalog and consultation-led path create more choices than a local owner initially needs.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Lead with the owner's question; introduce the relevant service only after useful guidance.

## 2. Pricing

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/pricing/)

**Observed:** Separates support tiers, website projects and marketing; lists monthly support at $99/$299/$799 and builds from $4,500.

**What works:** Public pricing makes initial qualification easier.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Different offers, billing periods and other pages' figures require reconciliation; a starting price is not a complete quote.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use one price configuration and show billing period, setup, exclusions and total commitment.

## 3. Website cost calculator

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/website-cost-calculator/)

**Observed:** A tested five-product/service scenario with copywriting and SEO, without ecommerce, produced $2,000–$4,500 before any contact details.

**What works:** Delivers real decision support before an optional exact-quote form.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The range needs assumptions, and its lower bound differs from the separate advertised build floor. Only one scenario was tested.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Adopt ungated estimating, adding understandable scope, recurring costs and an editable assumption summary.

## 4. Marketing budget calculator

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/marketing-budget-calculator/)

**Observed:** Introduces planning inputs and channel-budget education.

**What works:** A planning tool can make an abstract marketing discussion concrete.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The calculation was not executed; generic allocation guidance is not a recommendation for every local business.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Keep this outside the first release. If added, expose assumptions and let owners change them.

## 5. Website SEO audit service

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/website-seo-audit-service/)

**Observed:** Offers a paid audit with $300/$400/$500 options for different page allowances and additional optimization work.

**What works:** Deliverable, page coverage and expected turnaround make the paid work tangible.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The FAQ still describes $200 and $60/page while the main offer uses newer figures, creating a direct content inconsistency.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Separate the free automated check from paid manual depth and derive every price display from the same maintained source.

## 6. WordPress website design

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/wordpress-website-design-services/)

**Observed:** Explains managed design, content, optimization and project coordination followed by optional ongoing support.

**What works:** End-to-end responsibilities make the work easier to buy.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Design revision limits and later support promises can sound inconsistent unless the project stages are distinguished.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** State preview allowance, production revisions and ongoing change limits separately.

## 7. WordPress maintenance

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/wordpress-maintenance-services/)

**Observed:** Packages hosting, updates, backups and support, then higher levels of website work.

**What works:** Operational deliverables explain what ongoing fees purchase.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Urgent support availability and routine turnaround need separate expectations; uptime promises were not measured.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Describe response time, resolution expectations, coverage hours and exclusions distinctly.

## 8. Website management

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/website-management-services/)

**Observed:** Combines ongoing management with edit/project capacity and examples of migration or improvement work.

**What works:** Shows that care includes content and operations, not merely server hosting.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The annual dedicated-plan figure differs from the pricing page; trial wording also needs alignment with refund language.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Make terminology and current prices consistent across all pages; specify whether a trial is free or refundable.

## 9. Local SEO

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/local-seo-services/)

**Observed:** Describes a US/WordPress-oriented service with tiered monthly fees, setup charges and implementation dependencies.

**What works:** Eligibility and scope are more specific than a general promise to rank.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Several layers of fees and required support make total cost harder to understand.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Expose eligibility and all mandatory components together, while keeping optional enhancements separate.

## 10. FAQs

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/faqs/)

**Observed:** Selected sections clarify support response and turnaround, ownership, billing, complex-work exclusions and urgent requests.

**What works:** Detailed operational answers reduce post-sale surprises.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The large FAQ is not an efficient first explanation, and every answer was not individually audited.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Put essential limits beside the offer; use FAQs for details rather than hiding key conditions there.

## 11. Contact

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/contact-us/)

**Observed:** Requests contact/business information and project context, with a stated reply expectation.

**What works:** Provides a clear human-help route.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Retrieved content includes form variants; hidden or alternate values were not treated as visible UI defects. No request was sent.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use a short optional form after results, carrying forward already supplied context.

## 12. Consultation request

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/consultation-request/)

**Observed:** Offers an expert conversation and distinguishes prospective from existing customers.

**What works:** Useful routing can reduce unnecessary back-and-forth.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A consultation still requires contact and time; it is not equivalent to receiving an audit immediately.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Keep consultation available for interpretation or implementation after the free output.

## 13. Master services agreement

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/master-services-agreement/)

**Observed:** Selected clauses distinguish customer website assets from reusable provider components and describe termination, migration and scope-dependent compliance.

**What works:** Provides more precise ownership and service boundaries than marketing headlines.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The complete agreement was not legally reviewed; broad industry compliance language needs the scope qualifications nearby.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use clear original agreements and scope statements. Do not claim medical or accessibility compliance merely from design features.

## 14. Cancellation FAQ

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/cancellation-faq/)

**Observed:** Explains export/backup and optional paid migration, plus consequences for licensed components and hosted content.

**What works:** Exit logistics are unusually concrete.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Some assets or licenses do not transfer automatically; cancellation simplicity does not imply full migration is free.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** State what transfers, who performs migration, what costs extra and what stops working.

## 15. Testimonials

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/testimonials/)

**Observed:** Uses named clients, written accounts, video and external review references.

**What works:** Different proof formats can answer delivery and communication concerns.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Reviews were sampled for structure; videos and all claims were not independently verified.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Select a few relevant permissioned accounts rather than a large undifferentiated wall of praise.

## 16. Medical website design

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/medical-website-design/)

**Observed:** Covers patient-oriented information, appointment actions and trust, with medical design examples.

**What works:** The patient journey is more meaningful than generic agency feature lists.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Retrieved SEO copy refers to law firms/practices, suggesting template residue. Compliance claims need to match the actual agreed scope.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Create genuine profession-specific content; qualify medical data-handling requirements and avoid unsupported compliance promises.

## 17. Construction website design

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/construction-website-design/)

**Observed:** Groups construction examples by work type and emphasizes project evidence and local inquiry.

**What works:** Shows how visual proof and specific expertise can guide a prospect toward a quote.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Generic testimonials from unrelated industries weaken the otherwise targeted page.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use relevant project evidence and clearly described service areas, not industry-name substitution alone.

## 18. Healthcare provider / Burnalong case

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/case-studies/healthcare-provider-web-design/)

**Observed:** Describes an architecture serving employers, consultants, hospitals and patients with tailored actions and resources.

**What works:** A strong reference for organizing distinct audiences and tasks.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Design and content changes are described without a quantified business lift.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Explain the design rationale and keep unmeasured effects separate from observed outcomes.

## 19. MIS Solutions case

[Source page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/case-studies/mis-solutions-website-blueprint-for-msp-success/)

**Observed:** Describes a substantial website blueprint before production, with pricing/FAQ content, team photography and reusable content structures.

**What works:** Shows how strategic decisions become a concrete information architecture.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The planning engagement is substantial; its depth is not a promise of an instant free deliverable.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use a concise product spine now, then scale paid discovery to project complexity instead of offering unlimited free planning.

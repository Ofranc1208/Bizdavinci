# sitehike: page-by-page review

> Reference evidence (2026-10-23). For current product decisions and instructions, start with [the workspace documentation](../../../README.md). Observations, recommendations and vendor claims retain the qualifications below.

Reviewed October 23, 2026. 22 selected public pages. Each entry records retrieved content, a design judgment and a proposed Biz Da Vinci response. These are not measured conversion findings. Long testimonial collections, indexes and legal/FAQ pages were reviewed selectively as specified below. See [method](../METHOD.md), [site synthesis](README.md), [coverage](coverage.json) and [retrieval metadata](retrieval-metadata.json). Browser testing is limited to the states documented in the site synthesis.

## 1. Homepage

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/)

**Observed:** Connects websites, local search and customer follow-up with business outcomes and directs visitors toward work examples or a free audit.

**What works:** The relationship between finding a business and contacting it is easy to follow.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The homepage's hosted-site language requires reconciliation with the separate annual hosting charge. Published outcome metrics remain vendor claims.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Connect audit findings to customer actions while keeping hosting and other recurring charges explicit.

## 2. Pricing

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/pricing)

**Observed:** Advertises $347/$447/$697 monthly for the initial 12 months, lower ongoing rates afterward, and separate annual hosting; non-trade work can require custom pricing.

**What works:** Ownership timing and phased pricing answer real buying questions.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The annual hosting charge and industry applicability can be missed when reading the monthly price alone.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Show first-year commitment, ongoing care and ownership as separate facts; avoid implying every profession has identical scope.

## 3. Free audit

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/free-audit)

**Observed:** Offers a manually prepared report in about two business days covering local visibility, website issues and priorities. The inspected form requests contact details.

**What works:** A described deliverable and sample reduce uncertainty about what the owner receives.

**Friction or uncertainty:** It is a request-and-wait service rather than immediate ungated value. Real and fictional sample material must remain clearly distinguished.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Provide immediate evidence-backed findings and let deeper manual work be optional.

## 4. Services

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/services)

**Observed:** Organizes site work, local presence, SEO, follow-up and related services into a connected offering.

**What works:** Shows how services contribute to the overall customer journey.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Broad included-service language can imply more than the specific tier tables support.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use a simple outcome-led overview with an authoritative inclusion matrix underneath.

## 5. Industries

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/industries)

**Observed:** Covers exterior trades, other local services and additional sectors with industry-specific customer needs.

**What works:** The differences in proof, geography and buying intent are useful for multi-industry design.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A wide industry list alone cannot establish specialized delivery experience in every profession.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Share the underlying product flow while adapting examples, primary action and evidence to each business type.

## 6. Process

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/process)

**Observed:** Describes a roughly month-long path with early homepage review, subsequent build, launch and ongoing care.

**What works:** A homepage checkpoint and small stated owner time commitment reduce uncertainty.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The schedule depends on content and review readiness; minimal-discovery language does not eliminate required decisions.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Ask only what is needed, but show prerequisites and get approval before production.

## 7. FAQ

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/faq)

**Observed:** Clarifies ownership, hosting, terms, account access and qualified expectations for SEO; sometimes existing sites can be improved.

**What works:** Useful answers acknowledge that rankings cannot be guaranteed.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The ability to fix an existing site needs reconciliation with platform-specific maintenance/rebuild positioning.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** State when repair is supported, when migration is proposed and which accounts remain with the customer.

## 8. Contact

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/contact)

**Observed:** Provides audit, call and message routes; the message form requests business/contact context and indicates a one-business-day reply.

**What works:** Different levels of commitment let visitors choose how to engage.

**Friction or uncertainty:** It still places several fields before direct help; forms were inspected but not submitted.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Make human help available after free value, with a short context-aware form.

## 9. Book

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/book)

**Observed:** Offers a founder conversation with optional preparation and a promise against automatic follow-up sequences.

**What works:** Sets a more personal, lower-pressure expectation.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The calendar and meeting delivery were not tested, and a call is still a commitment.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Offer a conversation as a choice rather than the only way to understand a result.

## 10. Work index

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/work)

**Observed:** Groups examples by trades and beyond, with client links and project details; states that metrics require data and permission.

**What works:** Distinguishes demonstrable work from results that cannot responsibly be claimed.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Published examples still require attribution; neither live sites nor metrics were independently audited.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Adopt the proof discipline: concept, launched work and measured case should have different labels.

## 11. Reviews

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/reviews)

**Observed:** Connects customer comments with projects and cites Google feedback.

**What works:** Linking reviews to actual work provides more context than anonymous praise.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The review collection and its count were not independently authenticated.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use real permissioned feedback alongside the matching project, with original source links.

## 12. Website redesign

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/website-redesign)

**Observed:** Covers migration, redirect continuity, design and measured case examples.

**What works:** Recognizes that redesign must preserve working paths and search continuity.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A design/SEO bundle does not isolate design's effect; no migration can responsibly guarantee zero ranking risk.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Include URL inventory, redirects, existing booking/ordering preservation and post-launch checks in paid scope.

## 13. Website maintenance

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/website-maintenance)

**Observed:** Distinguishes platform upkeep from content changes and larger work, with platform-specific delivery.

**What works:** Makes ongoing responsibilities more concrete.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A rebuild may be needed outside the supported platform, which narrows the broader 'fix an existing site' promise.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Describe supported platforms and care limits before accepting a maintenance customer.

## 14. Local SEO

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/local-seo)

**Observed:** Explains listings, geographic visibility and relevant service/location content, including practical business-profile details.

**What works:** Uses specific local-business tasks rather than generic SEO jargon alone.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A page crawl cannot establish map rankings, calls or actual local market share.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Separate observable website checks from connected or externally measured search data.

## 15. CRM

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/crm)

**Observed:** Describes missed-call responses, shared conversations, booking, reviews and quote follow-up, with templates approved before activation.

**What works:** Connects website inquiries to the work required after a lead arrives.

**Friction or uncertainty:** These capabilities add operational responsibilities and consent/configuration needs; generic loss claims are not prospect-specific evidence.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Treat follow-up as a later optional service, with owner-approved messaging and real integration checks.

## 16. AI visibility

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/services/ai-visibility)

**Observed:** Provides dated search-answer examples and acknowledges answer variability.

**What works:** The qualification that AI answers change is more credible than an unconditional ranking promise.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A small prompt sample cannot establish broad visibility or business impact.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** If this is offered later, record prompts, date, sources and limits; never infer an AI ranking from HTML alone.

## 17. Restaurant website design

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/restaurant-website-design)

**Observed:** Adapts the proposition to menus, locations, events and catering while preserving ordering/POS tools.

**What works:** Strong example of tailoring the customer action without changing the entire product.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Its Stampede page count differs from the linked case, and restaurant pricing needs reconciliation with the general non-trade quote policy.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use profession-specific journeys and keep reusable facts in one source.

## 18. Virile / Stray Hare barber case

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/work/virile-barber-shop)

**Observed:** Describes a brand transition with booking continuity; the page explicitly notes the full site is not yet launched.

**What works:** Transparent project status prevents a concept from masquerading as a completed outcome.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Unlaunched work cannot substantiate conversion or revenue improvements.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Label design concept, in-progress work and launched results visibly.

## 19. Stampede BBQ case

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/work/stampede-bbq)

**Observed:** Describes a 35-page Squarespace project with two locations, menus, events and distinct inquiry routes.

**What works:** Demonstrates that the information architecture follows different visitor needs.

**Friction or uncertainty:** No quantified growth is established; the historical platform differs from the main current platform pitch.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Design around tasks such as menu, visit and catering; date case-study scope and platform.

## 20. Pelican Plumbing case

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/work/pelican-plumbing)

**Observed:** Describes a 14-page site with eight service pages and business-profile/review context.

**What works:** A useful example of service organization, trust and a clear inquiry path.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Review counts and visibility are vendor-reported context, not proof of redesign-caused revenue.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Show exactly which customer path improved and avoid converting a review count into a revenue claim.

## 21. Comparison with 180 Sites

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/compare/180-sites)

**Observed:** Compares pricing, term, ownership and service differences and acknowledges situations where staying with an existing provider is reasonable.

**What works:** Transparent fit criteria are more useful than blanket competitor criticism.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The comparison is written by an interested party; competitor details should be checked against primary pages.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Provide decision criteria and full costs; keep comparisons dated and independently sourced.

## 22. Terms of service

[Source page](https://www.sitehike.com/terms-of-service)

**Observed:** Describes the initial period, ownership after 12 payments, exit treatment, notice and excluded third-party costs; does not guarantee results.

**What works:** Clarifies why low initial cost and ownership timing are linked.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Early cancellation can end site availability even without further amounts owed. Customers need that distinction before choosing.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Explain cancellation payment, service shutdown, asset ownership and migration support separately.

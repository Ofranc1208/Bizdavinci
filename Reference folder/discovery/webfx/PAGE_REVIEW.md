# webfx: page-by-page review

> Reference evidence (2026-10-23). For current product decisions and instructions, start with [the workspace documentation](../../../README.md). Observations, recommendations and vendor claims retain the qualifications below.

Reviewed October 23, 2026. 15 selected public pages. Each entry records retrieved content, a design judgment and a proposed Biz Da Vinci response. These are not measured conversion findings. Long testimonial collections, indexes and legal/FAQ pages were reviewed selectively as specified below. See [method](../METHOD.md), [site synthesis](README.md), [coverage](coverage.json) and [retrieval metadata](retrieval-metadata.json). Browser testing is limited to the states documented in the site synthesis.

## 1. Website redesign services

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/web-design/services/redesign/)

**Observed:** A long proposal-led page combines portfolio, scope, calculator, process, ownership and case evidence. One calculator scenario returned $15,000–$25,500 without contact details.

**What works:** Provides both a usable estimate and substantial procurement information.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The free utility is far down the journey; technical calculator choices require expertise. The tested scope included ecommerce and other additions.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Place the estimator near the top and translate technical options into owner tasks; do not reuse the scenario as a simple brochure-site price.

## 2. Web design services

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/web-design/services/)

**Observed:** Describes custom design starting at $8,000, content and technical services, portfolio and typical delivery.

**What works:** Public scope and a starting price qualify the service before a proposal.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Many capabilities and broad performance claims can overwhelm a small-business visitor.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Keep detailed procurement material on supporting pages and summarize only the relevant recommended scope.

## 3. Redesign pricing guide

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/web-design/pricing/redesign/)

**Observed:** Provides a calculator, cost factors, provider comparisons and broad project ranges.

**What works:** Cost drivers help an owner understand why two projects differ.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Broad market ranges are not a binding WebFX quote; comparisons depend on scope and data quality.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Explain our own estimate inputs and assumptions instead of presenting generic market averages as a personalized price.

## 4. RainmakerFX rapid design

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/web-design/services/rainmakerfx/)

**Observed:** Offers a WordPress delivery path using five theme directions and three design hours, with 30 days counted from content receipt.

**What works:** Makes speed possible through bounded scope and prerequisites.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A shared portfolio block mentions a 12-week custom-build average alongside the rapid promise; readers need to distinguish products.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Tie turnaround promises to the selected scope, content readiness and review obligations.

## 5. Small-business web design

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/web-design/services/smb/)

**Observed:** Packages page setup, content, responsive design, SEO and integrations with a calculator and portfolio.

**What works:** Recognizes that small businesses have functional as well as aesthetic needs.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Much of the page resembles the wider enterprise service pitch; the stated custom starting price remains $8,000.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Adapt questions, examples and scope to local businesses instead of relabeling a large-service catalog.

## 6. Conversion design

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/web-design/services/conversion-design/)

**Observed:** Offers ongoing testing, reporting and UX/design work; the displayed base ongoing fee is $1,800 with separate setup.

**What works:** Treats improvement as measurement and iteration, not one decorative redesign.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Complex tier tables need traffic, goals and instrumentation context; listed tools were not independently verified as currently supported.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Recommend measurement before experimentation and distinguish evidence-based testing from a speculative concept.

## 7. Website management

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/web-development/services/website-management/)

**Observed:** Separates security, enhancements and proactive care, with pricing bands and detailed support/update allowances.

**What works:** Connects ongoing fees to explicit operations.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Large plans and detailed conditions can obscure which level a modest local site actually needs.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use a small care matrix with change allowance, coverage hours, security work and exclusions.

## 8. Design portfolio

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/portfolio/design-portfolio/)

**Observed:** A large collection spans construction, healthcare, local services, manufacturing and other sectors.

**What works:** Named examples demonstrate design range and can support industry relevance.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The index was sampled; every client website was not opened. An attractive design does not demonstrate causal performance lift.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Filter a small set by profession and desired customer action, then link each to a contextual case.

## 9. Cleveland Brothers case

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/portfolio/case-studies/cleveland-brothers/)

**Observed:** Describes consolidation of divisional sites, content and SEO, reporting an 82% organic traffic increase.

**What works:** Connects a specific structural problem to concrete implementation work.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The main reported traffic metric lacks a clear comparison period in the reviewed case; attribution spans multiple activities.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Show baseline, period, measurement source and which work happened together in any Biz Da Vinci case.

## 10. Reynolds case

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/portfolio/case-studies/reynolds/)

**Observed:** Describes design, SEO/PPC, content and behavior analysis; reports 71% year-over-year growth in organic contact submissions and 43% in organic traffic.

**What works:** The distinction between traffic and meaningful inquiries strengthens the proof story.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Year-over-year framing helps but no underlying analytics or causal isolation was available.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use outcome-specific measures with dates and explain the contribution of concurrent marketing work.

## 11. Enduric Plumbing case

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/portfolio/case-studies/enduric-plumbing/)

**Observed:** Connects service navigation, calls to action, copy and SEO with reported year-over-year lead and session growth.

**What works:** A relatable local-service example links customer tasks to measurement.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The vendor reports 120% more form leads and 303% more organic sessions; those results are not independently verified or transferable predictions.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Borrow the problem → change → measured outcome structure, never the numerical promise.

## 12. Reviews

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/about/reviews/)

**Observed:** Presents a very large testimonial collection and links examples to case studies.

**What works:** Named stories and linked context are stronger than isolated badges.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The collection was sampled for presentation, not exhaustively read or authenticated; aggregate results remain vendor claims.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Use a small number of relevant, attributable examples with honest scope.

## 13. Free quote

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/free-quote/)

**Observed:** The content describes a custom revenue plan with business/contact qualification and project-budget inputs.

**What works:** Appropriate detail can improve a serious proposal request.

**Friction or uncertainty:** A free proposal is a sales deliverable, not an immediate website diagnosis; form variants were not submitted or fully tested.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Make the proposal an optional continuation of a useful report, with existing context prefilled.

## 14. Contact

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/contact/)

**Observed:** Provides phone routes, office details and a general inquiry form; phone entry is accompanied by SMS-contact language.

**What works:** Multiple human channels and clear identity can reassure visitors.

**Friction or uncertainty:** Contacting the agency still requires a message or call; it is not an alternative to ungated analysis.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Provide human help without making it the sole path to value, and separate communication choices clearly.

## 15. Local SEO pricing

[Source page](https://www.webfx.com/local-seo/pricing/)

**Observed:** An educational guide discusses retainers, projects, locations and campaign scope, citing a survey of 250 US professionals.

**What works:** Methodology context and cost factors improve on an unexplained price claim.

**Friction or uncertainty:** The market figures are broad, and the calculator was not executed during this review.

**Biz Da Vinci implication:** Keep pricing education optional and distinguish market data, our configured rates and the customer's calculated estimate.

# Biz Da Vinci: five-site discovery

> Reference evidence (2026-10-23). For current product decisions and instructions, start with [the workspace documentation](../../README.md). Observations, recommendations and vendor claims retain the qualifications below.

Research date: October 23, 2026. This report informs product positioning and design; it is not an implemented website or approved rate card.

## The recommendation

Position Biz Da Vinci as a practical website-improvement service for local businesses: **understand what needs attention, see a better direction, and understand the likely cost before speaking to a salesperson.** Keep website production as managed delivery. Support multiple professions through relevant examples and customer actions.

The key product is a connected set of useful results. An audit explains a problem; a concept shows a possible improvement; an estimate explains the work and cost. Owners can enter through any of the three questions and keep the result without providing contact details.

The [detailed blueprint](BIZ_DA_VINCI_BLUEPRINT.md) defines layout, flow, result requirements, free-versus-paid boundaries, estimation rules and acceptance criteria. The updated [product spine](../PRODUCT_SPINE.md) preserves owner decisions, audience evidence and instructions for subsequent LLM work.

## What the five experiences teach us

| Reference | What works | What creates friction or needs qualification | Biz Da Vinci adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| [SiteFlip](https://www.siteflip.app/) | Visible transformation and a preview before commitment | The URL/email form promises a later preview; it does not deliver instant on-screen value | Show a bounded concept on-screen; clearly distinguish a concept from a production website |
| [180 Sites audit](https://180sites.com/audit) and [pricing](https://180sites.com/pricing) | Concrete packages, process and a deeper paid review | A free strategy call differs from an audit result; monthly price alone hides the scale of the term commitment | Free findings first; disclose setup, monthly fee, minimum term, ownership and total cost together |
| [SiteHike audit](https://www.sitehike.com/free-audit) and [industries](https://www.sitehike.com/industries) | Useful local-business framing and a described audit deliverable | Contact and waiting precede the personalized report; broad service promises need tier-specific limits | Immediate public-site findings, with profession-specific examples and honest unmeasured areas |
| [Pronto calculator](https://www.prontomarketing.com/website-cost-calculator/) and [pricing](https://www.prontomarketing.com/pricing/) | A range appears before contact details; build and ongoing care are distinguishable | Similar service names, varying scope and price differences across pages need explanation | A short business-language estimator, explicit assumptions and one maintained price source |
| [WebFX redesign](https://www.webfx.com/web-design/services/redesign/) | An ungated calculator, detailed scope and named case evidence | Long sales pages and technical inputs create effort for a local owner | Move utility to the top; use concise relevant proof and accessible supporting detail |

These are design judgments based on the observed journeys, not measured conversion-rate comparisons.

## Important corrections to the starting hypothesis

It would be inaccurate to claim that all five competitors demand contact details before offering useful information. Pronto and WebFX both produced an on-screen estimate before contact fields were completed. Biz Da Vinci's proposed distinction is the combined audit, concept and estimate experience with consistent no-gate delivery—not the invention of free calculators.

The tested Pronto scenario returned $2,000–$4,500; the tested WebFX scenario returned $15,000–$25,500. They had different inputs and scope, including ecommerce in the WebFX scenario. They are interaction evidence, **not an apples-to-apples price comparison** and not suggested Biz Da Vinci prices. Exact inputs and screenshots are in the individual reports.

An agency's free consultation is also different from a free audit report. The 180 Sites free audit route is a strategy call, with a paid deeper option. SiteHike describes a later emailed report. Pronto's dedicated SEO audit is a paid service. Our initial result should contain advice the owner can act on immediately.

## Consistency and trust lessons

The page review found several content-maintenance problems worth avoiding:

- The [180 Sites dental page](https://180sites.com/dental-web-design) contains differing prices and retrieved portfolio links to landscaping businesses. This is content/link evidence, not a complete browser defect test.
- The [Pronto audit page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/website-seo-audit-service/) has differing main-offer and FAQ prices. Its [medical page](https://www.prontomarketing.com/medical-website-design/) contains law-firm wording in an SEO section.
- [SiteFlip terms](https://www.siteflip.app/terms) distinguish subscription licensing from source-code acquisition, a nuance that should be visible beside ownership/export promises.
- [SiteHike pricing](https://www.sitehike.com/pricing) illustrates why hosting charges, initial payment periods and later care rates need to be shown together.
- [WebFX's Reynolds case](https://www.webfx.com/portfolio/case-studies/reynolds/) connects several services with reported outcomes. That is stronger context than an isolated percentage, but it still does not isolate redesign as the sole cause.

For Biz Da Vinci, maintain shared prices and service definitions; review profession-specific copy; label examples and unfinished concepts; and attach dates, sources and measurement limits to results. Do not borrow another agency's metrics as our promises.

## The homepage spine

Suggested headline: **What could your website do better?**

Supporting line: **Check it for free, explore a fresh direction, or see what improvements could cost.**

Place one action panel immediately below it:

| Top question | Minimal input | Useful free result | Optional paid continuation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What should I fix?** | Public website URL; clarify the business goal only if necessary | Evidence-linked findings, what already works and practical fixes | Have us implement selected improvements |
| **How could it look?** | Existing URL; optional goal/style preference | Labeled homepage concept with reasons for proposed changes | Approve a full managed redesign scope |
| **What would it cost?** | A few questions about pages/content, booking/orders, integrations and care | A range, assumptions, inclusions and one-time/ongoing costs | Request a precise proposal |

Use audit as the proposed default. Keep one active form and primary button; preserve context when switching routes. Show “Free results here. No email or phone required” only when the product genuinely delivers that behavior. A sample result lets a visitor explore without entering a URL.

Below the panel: a real example result → examples for different professions → how managed help works → clear service scope/pricing → relevant evidence and FAQs. Avoid a long wall of service cards before the first useful action. Give the owner a useful result before showing the optional contact form.

## Keep the free offer useful and deliverable

Offer a meaningful public-site review, a bounded homepage concept and a rule-based estimate. A full production website, unlimited revisions, migration, integrations and ongoing support are paid work. The boundary should describe scope honestly rather than conceal the useful recommendation behind a lead gate.

A public-page review cannot establish actual rankings, traffic, revenue loss or search-market share. Show what was checked and what remains unmeasured. For a blocked site, provide partial results or an alternative path instead of inventing a score. For slower concept generation, show useful available findings first and preserve a return link without forcing email.

Price calculation should use approved, versioned rules. The LLM may explain the result, but must not invent a quote. Determine delivery costs and margins before setting public Biz Da Vinci rates. Historical repository prices remain unvalidated proposals.

## How this fits the Tampa audience

The [business inventory](../TAMPA_BUSINESS_INVENTORY.md) contains 50 businesses across nine analytical groups. Use the same three-question experience for all, with different examples and customer actions: appointments for salons and practices, estimates for automotive/home services, menus/orders for food, visits and inquiries for retail/professional services.

Do not create nine unrelated homepages or automatically replace working booking/order tools. Do not treat a blank website cell as proof the business has no site. The first result examples should deliberately cover different professions so that the product does not silently become restaurant-only.

## Recommended next design artifact

Prototype the three-question homepage and the three result states, with an audit, concept and estimate linked by shared business context. Use representative examples from several professions and label illustrative content. Test whether owners understand the findings, price assumptions and optional paid next step before implementing a large agency website.

The [blueprint](BIZ_DA_VINCI_BLUEPRINT.md) provides the acceptance criteria for that work. A final visual direction, production implementation and public prices have not been selected in this research.
